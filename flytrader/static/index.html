    Trial settings

    Automatic mode rotates after this many observations or when prices go quiet. Pinned mode stays on your coin and keeps its neuron state. Active exploration uses 1% of cash per probe, up to 3% for learned entries, and chooses 30 / 60 / 180-second holds. Conservative mode uses up to 5% and holds for one price interval. All entries wait for the next fresh quote; risk or end-of-segment exits can shorten a hold. A 10% closed-trade drawdown pauses new entries.

    Exploration percentages apply to eligible decisions while in cash. Active mode also tries an initial probe and one after three eligible minutes without an entry. Market checks and cash limits still apply. Each training style and gain has separate saved memory.

    What is This?

    In short, it’s a “fly brain” that trades, and learns from trading. In more detail, it’s a hookup of a computational graph of the male fly’s nervous system, called MaleCNS, to activity on Pump.fun.

    166,700neurons
    25,582,938directed connections
    124,177,617synaptic contacts

    The nervous system updates on every observable price change, which is every 10 seconds, since pulling the data faster than that would get me rate limited. I could probably pull data faster, but for now, I think 10 seconds is fine. I modeled the reward and punishment system on how that works in flies, but whether this works better for this sort of task or not, I am not sure. This is something I will probably look more into, over time.

    The actual inside-out of how this works programmatically is dense, and I am a mere vibe coder. I had GPT Astra code out the interface between Pump.fun and MaleCNS. This is what it had to say:

    Full connectome: The default model contains 166,700 neurons, 25,582,938 directed connections, and 124,177,617 synaptic contacts. It retains every annotated neuronal superclass except glia and unresolved objects.

    Neural propagation: Every confirmed price observation updates all 166,700 neuron states. Activity travels along the real MaleCNS connection directions. Raw contact counts are normalized to keep the recurrent network stable, then passed through bounded tanh dynamics.

    Market input: Price movement, momentum, volume, market regime, and risk context are converted into artificial sensory signals. In the current reward model, these signals enter through real MaleCNS Kenyon cells, with about 5% activated for each observation.

    Decision circuit: The action model reads actual MBON states produced by the graph. It predicts the net return for 30, 60, and 180-second paper positions. Those estimates, exploration, market checks, liquidity limits, and rug-risk rules determine whether it paper-buys.

    Learning: Completed paper trades generate a prediction error. Profitable surprise activates the engineered PAM reward pathway; negative surprise activates PPL1. That signal modifies only connections that genuinely exist from KC neurons to MBON neurons in MaleCNS. The current full graph has 61,210 trainable KC→MBON connections.

    Visualization: The browser shows a fixed sample of 2,000 neurons because drawing all 166,700 would be impractical. Their positions come from MaleCNS soma coordinates, and their colors are real model states. The other neurons still compute even though they are not displayed.

    Reward and Punishment: In the fly mushroom body, sensory/context patterns are sparsely represented by Kenyon cells (KCs); reward-related PAM dopamine neurons and punishment-related PPL1 dopamine neurons modify different KC→MBON pathways. Reward tends to suppress avoidance output for the associated cue, while punishment suppresses approach output. The model emphasizes this circuitry, as opposed to using the CNS at random.

    The fly is not very good at trading at this time. I am not really thinking that the fly will ever become good at trading. If it did become good, I would be living quite comfortably. :)
